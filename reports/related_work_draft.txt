2. Related Work

Bangla fake news detection research has progressed through distinct methodological phases. Hossain et al. (2020) introduced BanFakeNews, the foundational annotated dataset (~50K articles, 97.4% authentic vs 2.6% fake), establishing that traditional ML (SVM with character n-grams, 91% F1) outperformed early neural models including multilingual BERT (68% F1), largely due to severe class imbalance. Subsequent work addressed this imbalance directly: Shibu et al. (2025) built BanFakeNews-2.0 (60K articles, 13K fake) and showed instruction-tuned LLMs (BLOOM 560M with QLoRA) reaching 89% macro-F1, while Roy et al. (2024) and George et al. (2025) explored recurrent and multichannel CNN-LSTM architectures on similarly sized datasets, reporting accuracies ranging from 75% to over 99% depending on preprocessing and balancing strategy.

More recent work has moved toward transformer fine-tuning specific to Bangla. Chowdhury et al. (2023) combined BanglaBERT with summarization and augmentation to handle the 512-token limitation, achieving 97% accuracy on longer articles. Bandan et al. (2024) directly compared BERT against RNN/LSTM/GRU variants, confirming BERT's superiority (95% vs <=94.6%) but did not probe deeper architectural additions on top of the transformer. Akther et al. (2025) took an alternative, training-free direction with a knowledge-driven authenticity-scoring approach (97.08% fake-detection accuracy), highlighting that non-neural, feature-based methods remain competitive for this task. Beyond text-only detection, BanMANI (2023) and Mishra et al. (2026) extended the problem to claim-level manipulation detection and multimodal (text+image) reasoning respectively, with Mishra et al. (2026) being the only prior work to apply rigorous statistical significance testing (paired t-test, Wilcoxon, McNemar) to an ablation study in this domain.

Research gap. Across this body of work, two gaps remain unaddressed. First, no prior study evaluates whether adding architectural complexity (e.g., CNN or attention layers) on top of a pretrained Bangla transformer yields a statistically verified improvement -- reported gains are typically presented as single-run point estimates without seed-robustness or significance testing, aside from Mishra et al. (2026), who use it in a multimodal, not purely textual, setting. Second, none of the reviewed Bangla fake-news studies formally test cross-source generalization; datasets are consistently split randomly (IID), leaving open the possibility that models exploit source-label shortcuts rather than genuine textual cues -- a risk made explicit by the fact that several fake-news sources in these datasets are drawn from a small number of fact-checking portals with perfectly separable labels. This study addresses both gaps directly, using a fixed BanglaBERT backbone with a multi-seed ablation (verified via repeated-measures ANOVA) and a source-held-out evaluation with bootstrap confidence intervals.
